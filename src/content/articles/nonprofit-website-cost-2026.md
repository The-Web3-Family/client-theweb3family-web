*By Christopher Clarke, founder of The Web3 Family, 20 years building websites · Inglewood, Los Angeles · Updated November 2026*

> **According to Project Cost Estimator (2026):** A small nonprofit website with up to 5 pages costs $3,750–$7,500 with a freelancer and $6,500–$13,000 with an agency.
>
> **According to me:** Small nonprofits can spend far less. A website is the starting point. The real value comes from showing up every week with new articles and videos that bring people to it.

## What does a small nonprofit website cost?

**According to <a href="https://projectcostestimator.com/nonprofit-website-cost" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Project Cost Estimator</a> (2026):** A small site with up to 5 pages costs $3,750–$7,500 with a freelancer and $6,500–$13,000 with an agency.

**According to me:** I charge my nonprofit clients as little as $200 for a website. A website is just the start, though. My goal is to grow each client into a monthly plan of $500 or more, because that's where the real work happens: articles that bring in search engine traffic and videos that bring in social media traffic. A website alone sits still. A website that's fed every week grows.

## Should a nonprofit pay monthly or all at once?

**According to <a href="https://www.hyperarts.com/nonprofit-website-cost/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">HyperArts</a> (2026):** Agency builds typically cost $15,000–$50,000 upfront, plus $1,500–$7,000 a year in upkeep.

**According to me:** I chose a monthly model on purpose. Work arrives at a steady, careful pace, so we grow wisely and nobody sits around waiting on a big launch. It also means every client benefits from everything I build. When I find a better tool or a better way to do something for one client, every client gets it. And honestly, a nonprofit can do a lot of this cheaper on their own with AI. The monthly plan is for those who'd rather have someone experienced doing it with them.

## Should a nonprofit build its own website?

**According to <a href="https://thenonprofitguide.com/guides/wix-for-nonprofits/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Nonprofit Guide</a> (2026):** Verified nonprofits get 70% off two years of Wix through TechSoup, and Squarespace offers 10% off the first payment.

**According to me:** I usually tell nonprofits they can build it themselves. Most people would rather spend their time on their mission than behind a computer, though, so I build it with them. I've been building websites for 20 years, so what takes a beginner weeks takes me days.

## Which donation platform keeps the most money?

**According to <a href="https://www.zeffy.com/blog/paypal-donation-fees-for-nonprofits" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Zeffy</a> (2026):** Zeffy passes the full $100 of a $100 gift to the nonprofit, funded by optional donor tips. Stripe's nonprofit rate keeps about $97.50.

**According to me:** I set my clients up on Zeffy. Based on all my research, it's the best option for small nonprofits right now. Many of my clients are funded through grants and other sources, so online donations are still growing for them. I'm also testing GiveSuite with one client for nonprofits that want more automation.

## What do nonprofits forget to budget for after launch?

**According to <a href="https://www.hyperarts.com/nonprofit-website-cost/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">HyperArts</a> (2026):** Ongoing upkeep runs $500–$2,400 a year with a freelancer, plus extras like photos and plugins.

**According to me:** Subscriptions. Nonprofits forget how fast software subscriptions add up, and a subscription that sits unused is wasted money. I use every tool I pay for to full capacity. When you work with me, you're getting the best tools on the market, and the cost of those tools is already included in your plan.
